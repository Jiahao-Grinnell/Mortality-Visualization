United States Mortality by Cause of Death, Race, and Gender

Mortality rates in the United States from 1969 to 2009, broken out by cause of death, race and gender. Build a comparison by adding up to eight series to the chart, then hover or use the arrow keys to read values year by year.

Deaths per 100,000 people — see “About these numbers” below

About these numbers

Units are unconfirmed. The visualization this page replaces carried no axis label, so the rates here are shown as deaths per 100,000 people — the usual convention for this source — but that has not been verified against the original extract, and it is not known whether the rates are age-adjusted. Confirm both before citing these figures.

Source
Mortality records from the National Center for Health Statistics, distributed by the National Bureau of Economic Research.
Coverage
Nine five-year snapshots: 1969, 1974, 1979, 1984, 1989, 1994, 1999, 2004 and 2009. The lines connect these observations; they are not annual data, and nothing is known about the years in between.
The 1999 break
The United States moved from ICD-9 to ICD-10 cause-of-death coding in 1999, marked on the chart. Several causes — particularly perinatal conditions and pregnancy and childbirth — jump sharply across that line. Those jumps are mostly reclassification, not a change in mortality, so comparisons spanning 1999 should be made with care.
Known data problems
In the 1999, 2004 and 2009 snapshots, many aggregate values fall outside the range of the components they summarise — which is arithmetically impossible. This affects 87 of 486 “All races” cells and 142 of 648 “All genders” cells, and it is confined to those three years. Treat the “All races” and “All genders” figures from 1999 onward as unreliable pending a corrected extract; the individual race and gender series are unaffected.
Reading the chart
Switch the vertical axis to Log when comparing causes of very different size — rates here span roughly 0.1 to 400 per 100,000, and a linear axis flattens everything below the largest series.
